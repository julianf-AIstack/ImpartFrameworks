Privacy — plainly

Last updated 1 August 2026

This tool sends nothing, anywhere, to anyone.

There is no sign-up, no account, no cookie, no analytics, and no tracking. It does not ask for your name or your email. Everything you tap or type stays on the device you are using and goes nowhere else.

So where do my answers go?

Into your own browser's storage, on your own device — the same place a website remembers your dark-mode preference. Specifically it keeps:

  • Your answers, including anything you write in the text boxes
  • Your progress, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off
  • Your past results — up to 24 of them — so you can see change over time

Nobody else can read any of it. Not Impart, not your employer, not anyone running this site. It never travels across the internet, so there is nothing to intercept and nothing sitting on a server somewhere with your name on it.

To erase it, clear your browser data for this site, or use your browser's private/incognito window so nothing is kept once you close it. That is immediate and complete, and it doesn't involve us — because we were never holding it.

Don't take our word for it — here's how to check

Anyone can verify this in about a minute, no technical background needed:

  1. Right-click this page (or the audit) and choose View Page Source. You are looking at the entire program. There is nothing hidden — it's one file.
  2. Press F12 (or ⌥⌘I on a Mac) to open your browser's developer tools, click the Network tab, then take the whole audit. It will stay empty. That tab lists every single thing a page sends or receives.
  3. Turn off your wi-fi entirely and take the audit anyway. It works perfectly, because it never needed a connection in the first place.

There is also a rule built into the page itself, called a Content Security Policy, which instructs your browser to block this page from making any network request at all. So even if there were a bug in our code, or someone tampered with it, your browser would refuse to let anything out. That is a guarantee enforced by your browser, not a promise made by us.

The word "private", since it comes up

Two different meanings, and neither one should worry you:

  • Your answers are private — meaning yours alone, visible only to you, on your device. We are not asking you for private information and we could not collect it if we wanted to.
  • The framework's working notes are kept in a private repository — meaning an ordinary, non-public folder of Impart's own drafts and business material. It contains nothing about you. It exists because the underlying framework is Impart's own work, not because anything of yours is stored there.

If this ever changes

Not today, and not without asking you first. Impart may eventually add a genuinely optional button that lets you contribute only your four percentage scores — the numbers, nothing else — to help build research on which of the four elements people most commonly struggle with.

If that is ever added, it will be off by default, it will require a deliberate tick and click, skipping it will never cost you any part of your result, and this page will be rewritten to describe exactly what is sent before it goes live. Anything you have written would still never be included — that is a fixed design decision, not a setting.

Until you see that described here in the present tense, none of it exists.

What this is not

The Belief Systems Audit is a workforce-development diagnostic — a thinking tool about your career. It is not therapy, not a mental health assessment, and not a clinical instrument. It does not diagnose anything, and no result from it is a medical opinion.

The framework carries this warning openly: trauma and fear can install a belief instantly, bypassing the process this audit measures. Where that is what's happening, healing comes before belief work, and that is a job for a licensed professional rather than a questionnaire.

If you are in crisis, please contact a qualified professional or an emergency service in your country.
